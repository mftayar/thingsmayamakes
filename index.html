Words excite local wordsmith

Campaigns, websites, patient education, and brand stories for health, pharma, and purpose-led brands. Complex ideas, simple stories; one word at a time.

By Maya Franco-TayarPortland, OregonEst. reading time: one portfolio

PORTLAND, Ore. — For 11 years, Maya Franco-Tayar has turned scientific jargon into stories people can connect with, writing for companies including Eli Lilly, Novartis, Pfizer, and CeraVe.

Her work spans patient brochures and DTC materials, unbranded awareness films, campaign concepts, websites, and the occasional podcast email.

Asked what drives her, she pointed to the words themselves: the never-ending patterns that transform understanding, and the simplicity of a sentence that sparks imagination.

Profile

Hello!

My name is Maya Franco-Tayar. I am a freelance writer, avid adventurer, ocean addict, and soup savant with a passion for storytelling.

I have 11 years of experience crafting compelling and effective messaging for diverse audiences and transforming scientific jargon into engaging stories people can connect with.

Words excite me. Maybe it’s the never-ending patterns that transform understanding, the simplicity of a sentence that sparks imagination, or the dependency of each word on another to form context. Whatever it is, I wield them to create everything from concepts to campaigns, headlines to websites, copy for treatments, awareness initiatives, articles, blog posts, podcast summaries, ads, social media, and content for entrepreneurs across industries.

I have worked remotely for over 6 years, and often find inspiration in the culture and people around me. As a wandering wordsmith, I am spurred with curiosity about the impact of storytelling on societal and individual development. This insatiable interest has led me around the globe, backpacking through Southeast Asia, South America, Europe, and Australia. Although my spontaneity has sometimes gotten me in trouble, I’ve found that it only leads to good stories. And I love a good story. Because after all, when it comes to engaging an audience, it all starts with a little imagination—and a well-woven tale.

The next epic story is just an email away.
